Abstract

This continuously-updated repository presents a retrospective on the AI and semiconductor hardware stack across ten sectors: GPUs, CPUs, memory, chip fabrication, AI accelerators, advanced packaging, photonics, interconnects, datacenter hardware, and edge AI hardware. Approximately 230 primary sources published between November 2025 and May 2026 are validated against six recency, methodology, and attribution criteria. A cross-sector permutation pass produces ranked non-consensus findings, each of which is then independently checked against current sell-side, vendor, and trade-press consensus by a separate verification agent. The pipeline re-executes every five hours; already-priced-in findings are cleanly removed from the synthesis to preserve signal. Of the eighteen claims scrutinized in Run #1, two cleanly survived as genuinely non-consensus: (i) the PCIe 7.0 compliance slip displaces the CXL 4.0 memory-wall fix to approximately 2029, and (ii) compute-grade HBM (CG-HBM) together with CXL 4.0 disaggregation jointly attack per-GPU silicon interposer demand. Nine further claims are partially priced; seven were retired as already absorbed by mainstream analyst coverage. The methodology, audit trail, and complete sector reports are reproduced in the sections that follow.

Methodology

The pipeline operates in three stages. Stage 1: ten parallel sector teams each execute a four-agent chain (Collector → Deep Analyzer → Validator → Writer) over a six-month recency window, producing per-sector sources, paper analyses, validation logs, and a synthesis. Stage 2: after a hard synchronization barrier, a Global Synthesis pass writes the conclusion, market-opportunity, future-trends, and cross-sector alpha files. Stage 3: an independent Market-Pricing Verification Agent reads the synthesis and performs fresh web searches against sell-side notes, specialist research (SemiAnalysis, Epoch AI, The Next Platform), trade press, and vendor disclosures, assigning each non-consensus claim one of VERIFIED-NOT-PRICED-IN, PARTIALLY-PRICED-IN, or ALREADY-PRICED-IN, with dated evidence URLs. Already-priced-in items are then deleted from the synthesis files; the verification log retains the complete audit trail.
