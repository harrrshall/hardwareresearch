## Abstract / Summary

TSMC's A16 (1.6nm-class) process node is the first TSMC node to incorporate backside power delivery, branded as "Super Power Rail" (SPR). Targeting Q4 2026 for production entry (with volume HVM in 2027), A16 uses optimized nanosheet GAA transistors combined with SPR to achieve the first major IR-drop reduction in TSMC's commercial process history. A16 marks the start of TSMC's "Angstrom Era," a series of sub-2nm nodes prefixed with "A."

## Key Technical Data

- **Node designation:** A16 (1.6nm class; marketing name)
- **Transistor architecture:** Optimized GAAFET nanosheet (same foundation as N2, refined)
- **Backside power technology:** Super Power Rail (SPR) — TSMC's first BSPDN
- **Performance vs. N2P:** 8-10% speed increase at same power/area
- **Power vs. N2P:** 15-20% reduction at same frequency and transistor count
- **Density vs. N2P:** 1.07x–1.10x logic density improvement
- **SRAM density:** 8-10% improvement vs. N2P
- **SPR contact:** Dedicated backside contact to source and drain of each transistor
- **Production target:** Q4 2026 (risk); HVM Q1-Q2 2027
- **Primary customers:** High-performance data center and AI applications

## Key Findings

1. A16's "Super Power Rail" routes Vdd/Vss through silicon backside, eliminating power routing congestion on metal layers 1-4.
2. SPR reduces IR drop, enabling more aggressive voltage scaling and higher frequency operation at lower leakage.
3. TSMC moved SPR from N2P (originally planned) to A16, allowing N2P to focus on density/performance without BSPDN complexity.
4. A16 is targeted at data center AI accelerators (not mobile), where power delivery efficiency is the primary bottleneck.
5. The A-series (A16, A14, A13, A12) represents TSMC's roadmap through 2029, each iteration adding density and power improvements.
