## Abstract / Summary

Intel's 14A process node (~1.4nm class) is positioned as the first commercial semiconductor process to use High-NA EUV lithography for production patterning. 14A features second-generation backside power delivery (PowerDirect) and an evolved RibbonFET 2 transistor. Early PDK access has been provided to select customers in 2025-2026, with production targeting ~2027. The system cost for High-NA EUV (>$350M per tool) creates significant barriers to entry.

## Key Technical Data

- **Process node class:** 14A (~1.4nm marketing name)
- **Lithography:** High-NA EUV (ASML EXE:5200/EXE:5200B, 0.55 NA) — first commercial production use
- **Transistor:** RibbonFET 2 (improved 2nd-gen GAA)
- **Power delivery:** PowerDirect (2nd-generation BSPDN; improved vs. 18A PowerVia)
- **High-NA EUV tool cost:** >$350 million per system
- **PDK availability:** Early access provided to 2+ prospective foundry customers (2025-2026)
- **Production target:** ~2027
- **Customer requirement:** Requires firm foundry customer decision H2 2026 for 2027 tape-out
- **18A-P (bridge node):** Performance variant of 18A; ~18% power savings vs. 18A; mass production late 2026

## Key Findings

1. 14A is the first node where High-NA EUV is necessary (not optional) — TSMC and Samsung will also need High-NA EUV from A14 / SF1.4 onwards.
2. PowerDirect 2nd-gen BSPDN improves on PowerVia by reducing interconnect resistance and enabling tighter source/drain contacts to the backside rail.
3. The >$350M tool cost means only large foundries with sustained HVM demand can amortize High-NA EUV — a structural barrier to new entrants.
4. Intel has confirmed 18A-P as the performance variant bridging 18A and 14A — targeting HPC and AI workloads requiring maximum performance before 14A availability.
5. First High-NA EUV acceptance was completed end of 2025; Intel operates multiple EXE:5000 systems at Hillsboro for process development.

## Relevance to Research Window (2025-11-22 to 2026-05-22)

Intel 14A PDK distribution to customers is occurring during the research window. 18A-P early wafers are running through fabs. Intel presented 18A-P vs. TSMC A16 at VLSI 2026 (May 2026), at the end of the research window.
