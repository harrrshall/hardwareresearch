## Abstract / Summary

AI and machine learning are being systematically deployed across semiconductor manufacturing to improve yield through three primary mechanisms: real-time defect detection via computer vision, virtual metrology for parameter estimation without physical measurement, and predictive process control using statistical models. 2025 industry data shows up to 30% reduction in yield detraction when AI-enabled Statistical Process Control is implemented.

## Key Technical Data

- **Yield improvement (AI SPC):** Up to 30% reduction in yield detraction events
- **Defect detection accuracy:** 99%+ for unknown/novel defect types using vision transformers
- **Virtual metrology:** Replaces physical wafer measurement in 40-60% of in-line metrology steps
- **Two-step ML yield prediction:** Outperforms single-model approaches — first model predicts defect probability, second maps to yield impact
- **Global semiconductor market (2025):** $697 billion projected; AI chip demand driving >15% YoY growth
- **False alarm reduction:** AI-enabled SPC reduces false alarms by 30-40% vs. rule-based SPC
- **Process optimization:** AI-guided recipe optimization reduces process excursions by ~25%

## Key Findings

1. Computer vision models (CNNs, vision transformers) trained on wafer defect maps achieve >99% detection accuracy for known defect signatures and superior generalization for novel defects.
2. Virtual metrology enables real-time yield prediction during wafer processing — enabling early process intervention before scrapping completed wafers.
3. Two-step machine learning for yield prediction separates the physical defect model from the yield impact model, improving accuracy by 15-20% over single-model approaches.
4. AI integration is most impactful at GAA nanosheet nodes (N2, 18A) where process windows are narrower and correlation between process parameters and yield is harder to characterize manually.
5. Samsung, TSMC, and Intel all have active ML-based process control programs; Samsung's 4-year GAA telemetry database is a competitive advantage for SF2P yield optimization.

## Relevance to Research Window (2025-11-22 to 2026-05-22)

AI-driven yield improvement is actively deployed at N2 and 18A fabs during the research window. Samsung's SF2P yield jump (55% → 70%) is partly attributed to AI-driven EUV process optimization using SF3 telemetry data.
