## Abstract / Summary

Rapidus, Japan's government-backed foundry startup, activated its 2nm GAA pilot line at Chitose (Hokkaido) in April 2025, achieving the first successful operation of 2nm GAA transistors on July 18, 2025. The process is based on IBM's research process and uses High-NA EUV lithography. Rapidus secured $1.7 billion in new funding (early 2026) and reports active discussions with 60+ prospective customers.

## Key Technical Data

- **Pilot line activation:** April 2025 at IIM-1 facility, Chitose, Hokkaido
- **First 2nm GAA transistors:** July 18, 2025
- **Process basis:** IBM 2nm GAA research process
- **Lithography:** High-NA EUV (ASML EXE:5000 deployed at site)
- **Throughput model:** Single-wafer processing (unique vs. batch processing at TSMC/Samsung)
- **Funding raised (early 2026):** $1.7 billion (government + private investors)
- **Prospective customers:** 60+ in AI, robotics, edge computing
- **Mass production target:** 2027
- **Advanced packaging:** Trial production scheduled April 2026
- **FY2026 NEDO budget:** Approved for 2nm integration technologies + chiplet package design

## Key Findings

1. Rapidus is the only new entrant at the leading edge since GlobalFoundries exited sub-10nm competition in 2018.
2. Single-wafer processing approach enables custom lot tracking and short turnaround time (TAT) — targeting fabless customers who need fast design iteration.
3. High-NA EUV deployment is a leap-frog strategy — skipping standard EUV to start with the most capable lithography available.
4. The IBM process partnership provides a proven device architecture but does not guarantee production yield maturity.
5. If Rapidus reaches 2027 mass production targets, Japan could capture ~20% of leading-edge logic market by early 2030s.
