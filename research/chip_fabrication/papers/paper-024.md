## Abstract / Summary

At VLSI 2026 (May 2026), Intel and TSMC each presented their next-generation process node advances in a direct competitive showdown: Intel's 18A-P (performance-enhanced 18A) against TSMC's A16 (Super Power Rail debut). Intel claims 18% power savings for 18A-P vs. 18A baseline; TSMC presents A16 SPR architecture enabling 8-10% speed improvement or 20% power reduction over N2P. The conference marks the first public comparative disclosure of both next-generation nodes.

## Key Technical Data

**Intel 18A-P:**
- Power savings vs. 18A baseline: ~18%
- Architecture: Enhanced PowerVia routing + optimized RibbonFET Vt tuning
- Production target: Late 2026
- Foveros Direct 3D: Available in 18A-PT variant

**TSMC A16 (SPR Debut):**
- Speed vs. N2P: +8-10% at iso-power
- Power vs. N2P: -15-20% at iso-frequency
- BSPDN brand: Super Power Rail (SPR)
- Density vs. N2P: +7-10%
- SRAM density: +8-10% vs. N2P
- Production target: Q4 2026 risk; HVM 2027

**VLSI 2026 Conference:**
- Theme: "Advancing the AI Frontier Through VLSI Innovation"
- Timing: Aligns with the final date of the research window (May 22, 2026)

## Key Findings

1. Intel and TSMC are within ~2% of each other on power efficiency claims at their respective next-gen nodes — the gap has narrowed substantially from 2024 when Intel was clearly trailing.
2. TSMC's A16 SPR is the first full BSPDN implementation from TSMC, while Intel's PowerVia is already shipping since 18A — Intel has a ~1 year production deployment lead.
3. Both nodes target the 2026-2027 production window for AI data center applications; mobile clients will continue on N2P and 18A-P.
4. VLSI 2026 marks the first time three companies (Intel, TSMC, Samsung) all present competing GAA + BSPDN process nodes at the same venue.
5. The performance parity signals that the "process leadership" narrative is shifting from single-foundry dominance to a competitive multi-foundry landscape.
