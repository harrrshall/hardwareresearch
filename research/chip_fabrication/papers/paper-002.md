## Abstract / Summary

Intel's 18A process node integrates two first-of-kind technologies: 2nd generation RibbonFET gate-all-around transistors and PowerVia backside power delivery network. The node targets the leading-edge competitive landscape at ~1.8nm equivalent density. Intel began risk production at its Oregon Fab in late 2024 and entered high-volume manufacturing in October 2025, with Panther Lake (Core Ultra 300-series) as the lead product.

## Key Technical Data

- **Transistor architecture:** RibbonFET (Gate-All-Around, ribbon/nanosheet geometry)
- **Power delivery:** PowerVia (backside power delivery — industry first commercial deployment)
- **Density gain:** 30% vs. Intel 3 node
- **Performance:** 25% faster at iso-power vs. Intel 3; or 36% lower power at same clocks/1.1V
- **PowerVia benefit:** 5-10% better standard cell utilization; up to 4% ISO-power performance improvement
- **Yield (2025):** 60-65% at volume ramp; monthly improvement rate ~7%
- **Yield target:** Industry-standard level (~85%) targeted end 2026; definitive by 2027
- **MIM capacitors:** Ferroelectric HfZrO (HZO) stacks demonstrated at IEDM 2025 for decoupling

## Key Findings

1. 18A is Intel's first simultaneous deployment of BSPDN and GAA in a single production node.
2. PowerVia routes all power through silicon backside, freeing front-side routing for signal wires — enabling higher logic density.
3. Panther Lake CPUs produced on 18A represent the first commercial silicon with both GAA and BSPDN.
4. Yield trajectory: from ~25-30% initial to 60-65% by Q4 2025 via ~7%/month improvement cadence.
5. 18A-P (performance variant) and 18A-PT (with Foveros Direct 3D stacking) announced for 2026.

## Relevance to Research Window (2025-11-22 to 2026-05-22)

Intel 18A entered high-volume manufacturing October 2025, directly within the research window. Panther Lake debuted at CES 2026. Yield stabilization and 18A-P ramp are the primary focus through H1 2026.
