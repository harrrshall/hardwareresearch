## AI-Specific Cost Premium

- Standard hyperscale fit-out: $10.7–11.3M per MW
- AI-specific infrastructure (liquid cooling, HVDC): up to $25M per MW for full fit-out
- Tenant tech fit-out (GPU hardware): $25M+ per MW additional

## Full Project Cost Model

### Standard 100 MW Hyperscale
- Shell and core: $1.07–1.13 billion
- Full fit-out (electrical, cooling, fire suppression): $1.6–2.2 billion total
- Per MW all-in: $16–22 million

### AI-Optimized 100 MW Facility
- Construction alone: $2 billion+
- Full project (with equipment): significantly higher

### Gigawatt Campus
- Fully built with power, land, connectivity, equipment: $45–55 billion per GW

## Cost Driver Analysis

1. **Electrical gear supply constraint:** 2–3 year lead time for large transformers (2025)
2. **Liquid cooling infrastructure:** CDUs, manifolds, chilled water plants premium 40–60% over air-cooled
3. **Structural:** Floor loading for 1.36 metric ton GB200 NVL72 racks requires reinforcement
4. **Construction labor:** Specialized crews for liquid cooling systems in short supply
5. **Power acquisition:** Grid interconnection costs embedded in site selection premium

Cost increase drivers: thousands of watts per GPU crossing 1 kW threshold; liquid cooling systems; NVLink switch hardware.

## Market Volume

- Hyperscaler CapEx combined: hitting $600 billion in 2026 (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon)
- Data center infrastructure market: on path to $1 trillion total spending by 2030
- Construction starts: record activity 2025–2026 in US, EU, Middle East, Southeast Asia

## Site Selection Criteria (2025–2026 Priority Order)

1. **Available megawatts** (grid connection + substation headroom) — dominant constraint
2. Land availability (1,000+ acres for GW campus)
3. Water availability (cooling water source)
4. Fiber connectivity (low-latency to major internet exchange points)
5. Permitting environment (state/local regulatory speed)
6. Tax incentives (state data center incentive programs)
7. Labor market (construction and operations workforce)

## Implications

The per-MW cost doubling (from $7.7M in 2020 to $11.3M in 2026) reflects both AI infrastructure premium and supply chain tightness. The fundamental economic constraint shifting from compute cost to power availability means operators with existing substation connections have a multi-year advantage over new entrants.
