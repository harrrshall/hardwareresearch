## One-Sentence Claim
Kioxia presented a highly stackable oxide-semiconductor channel transistor technology at IEDM 2025 that enables a route to high-density, low-power 3D DRAM by leveraging the semiconductor's low leakage properties for vertical integration.

## Methodology Summary
Kioxia's approach uses oxide-semiconductor (OS) channel transistors — analogous to the IGZO work at imec — but focuses on the stackability dimension: demonstrating multi-layer vertical integration of the OS transistors for 3D DRAM arrays. The technology provides low off-state current (essential for data retention without a capacitor or with a minimal capacitor).

## Quantitative Results
- Approach: oxide-semiconductor channel transistors for 3D DRAM vertical integration
- Low off-current: enables sufficient retention time for DRAM operation
- Density improvement pathway: 3D stacking × layer count, similar to 3D NAND model
- Power advantage: low leakage current reduces standby power vs. conventional DRAM

## Stated Limitations
- Technology presented at academic/research conference; no production timeline provided
- Specific process node or minimum feature size not publicly disclosed from the session
- Reliability data (retention, endurance, temperature cycling) only partially reported

## Inferred Limitations
- OS channel transistors have lower carrier mobility than Si, potentially limiting access speed
- Stackability demonstration at IEDM may be at small-scale prototype level, not wafer-scale integration
- OS 3D DRAM faces same competitive pressure as IGZO from conventional DRAM vendors defending planar scaling

## Architectural Significance
Kioxia's contribution at IEDM 2025 is significant as it comes from a major NAND flash manufacturer, signaling that NAND producers see 3D-DRAM as a potential expansion opportunity leveraging their 3D stacking expertise. The oxide-semiconductor approach borrows from the 3D NAND playbook, which successfully stacked 400+ layers using similar vertical integration techniques.
