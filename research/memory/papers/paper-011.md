## One-Sentence Claim
Samsung and SK Hynix are jointly standardizing LPDDR6-PIM through JEDEC for 2026 products, while SK Hynix's deployed AiMX (GDDR6-AiM) card demonstrates 10x LLM inference speedup at 1/5 power vs GPU, and arXiv research proves functionally-complete bulk-bitwise computation in unmodified commercial DRAM chips.

## Methodology Summary
SK Hynix's GDDR6-AiM integrates MAC (multiply-accumulate) units directly into GDDR6 dies, operating at 16 Gbps. The AiMX product is a PCIe card containing multiple GDDR6-AiM chips. Samsung and SK Hynix are collaborating on JEDEC standardization of LPDDR6-PIM, expected to receive approval in 2026. Independent researchers (arXiv 2412.19275) demonstrated bulk-bitwise operations on unmodified commercial DRAM.

## Quantitative Results
- GDDR6-AiM speed improvement vs CPU/DRAM: up to 16x
- GDDR6-AiM power reduction vs GPU: 80%
- AiMX LLM processing time reduction vs GPU: >10x
- AiMX power vs GPU: 1/5
- GPT-1.3B benchmark: 347.83 tokens/s (8x PIM bandwidth scaling), 1.6x faster than NVIDIA A100
- Energy efficiency: 306.7 GOPS/W in 8-bit matrix multiplications (near-memory variant)
- LPDDR6-PIM: targeting JEDEC approval 2026

## Stated Limitations
- AiMX is optimized specifically for attention operations; general-purpose compute tasks are not its target
- GDDR6-AiM bandwidth (16 Gbps × limited die count) cannot match HBM4 aggregate bandwidth
- LPDDR6-PIM standard not yet finalized; product availability is 2026 at earliest

## Inferred Limitations
- PIM approaches break the separation between memory and compute, complicating programmability and debugging
- Heat generated by in-memory MAC units affects data retention and may require modified refresh schemes
- Industry adoption requires software stack changes (custom libraries, compiler support) that slow deployment
- LLM attention optimization is valuable but represents only one phase of the LLM inference pipeline

## Architectural Significance
The convergence of two major memory vendors (Samsung and SK Hynix) on a JEDEC LPDDR6-PIM standard is historically significant — it signals that PIM is transitioning from research to standardized commodity. The AiMX deployments have validated the architectural concept in production LLM serving environments. The arXiv research showing bulk-bitwise computation in unmodified DRAM chips opens a path to PIM without custom silicon.
