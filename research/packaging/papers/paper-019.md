# paper-019: Geopolitics and Advanced Packaging Supply Chain — US-Taiwan Diversification

**Tags:** geopolitics, supply-chain, advanced-packaging, TSMC, Intel, US-expansion
**Date Range:** 2025-Q4 – 2026-Q2
**Source IDs:** 56

---

## Summary

Advanced packaging supply chain is concentrated in Taiwan, which produces ~85% of the world's advanced AI semiconductors. Geopolitical pressure from Taiwan Strait tensions and US CHIPS Act incentives are driving TSMC ($165B Arizona commitment), Intel (Rio Rancho packaging ramp), SK Hynix ($13B HBM packaging plant in Indiana), and Amkor (Arizona campus) to build US-based advanced packaging capacity.

## Technical Details

**Taiwan Concentration Risk:**
- Taiwan: ~85% of advanced AI semiconductor production (logic + packaging)
- Taiwan: ~60% of global chip supply overall
- TSMC CoWoS: >95% of all 2.5D AI chip packaging worldwide
- Single-point failure scenario: Taiwan conflict = near-total AI chip production halt

**US Advanced Packaging Investments (Announced 2025–2026):**
| Company | Facility | Technology | Timeline | Investment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSMC | Arizona P6 (repurposed) | Advanced packaging | Equipment ~2027 | Part of $165B |
| TSMC | Arizona existing | CoWoS (limited) | 2025 ramp | — |
| Intel | Rio Rancho (Fab 9, 11x) | Foveros Direct + EMIB | 2025 active | — |
| SK Hynix | Indiana | HBM packaging | 2027 plant; approved Jan 2026 | $13B |
| Amkor | Arizona | OSAT (EMIB-outsourced) | 2025 active (Songdo K5) | — |

**US-Taiwan Trade Agreement (January 2026):**
- US and Taiwan signed agreement including $250B in direct Taiwanese semiconductor investments in the US
- Goal: Bring 40% of Taiwan's semiconductor supply chain to the US
- Includes packaging facilities as a critical component

**CHIPS Act Impact:**
- Incentivizes advanced packaging R&D and manufacturing (alongside logic fabs)
- Arizona is emerging as a geographic hub: TSMC + Intel + Amkor + ON Semi all present

## Key Findings

1. The January 2026 US-Taiwan agreement on $250B semiconductor investment is a landmark geopolitical event for packaging supply chain.
2. SK Hynix's $13B Indiana HBM packaging plant specifically targets US-sovereign HBM supply — NVIDIA's Rubin supply chain could be partially de-risked.
3. Intel's Fab 9 and 11x in Rio Rancho are the first US sites mass-producing 3D advanced packaging (Foveros Direct, EMIB).
4. TSMC's Arizona P6 repurpose from logic fab to advanced packaging signals the scarcity of CoWoS capacity is viewed as a national security issue.
5. Despite all investments, meaningful US advanced packaging volume is 3–5 years away from significant Taiwan supply chain offset.

## Implications

Geopolitical risk is now a packaging technology driver, not just a business consideration. Companies specifying AI hardware must factor in packaging geographic diversification in procurement strategy. The 5-year buildout lag means the 2026–2030 window remains critically dependent on Taiwan for advanced packaging capacity.
